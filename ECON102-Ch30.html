The Federal Budget, Fiscal Policy & Key Institutions

The federal budget is the annual statement of the federal government's outlays and tax revenues. It has two purposes: 1. To finance federal government programs and activities, and 2. To achieve macroeconomic objectives.

Fiscal policy is the use of the federal budget to achieve macroeconomic objectives such as full employment, sustained economic growth, and price level stability.

• Institutions: The President and Congress make fiscal policy.
• Employment Act of 1946: Congress declared it the continuing policy and responsibility of the Federal Government to use all practicable means to promote maximum employment, production, and purchasing power.
• Council of Economic Advisers (CEA): Monitors the economy and keeps the President and public informed about the current state of the economy and best available forecasts to inform the budget process.

Budget Balance Formula & Fiscal Year 2022 Timeline

• Budget surplus: Receipts > Outlays (government makes loan repayments).
• Balanced budget: Receipts = Outlays.
• Budget deficit: Outlays > Receipts (government borrows to cover the shortfall).

2. March – September 2021: Congress debates, amends, and enacts the budget.

4. Sept 30, 2022: Fiscal year 2022 ends (accounts are prepared; outlays, receipts, and budget deficit are reported).

Receipts, Outlays & Deficits in Historical Perspective

• Persistent Deficit: Except for four years around 2000 (when the budget was in surplus), the U.S. federal budget has been in a persistent deficit.
• 2008 Financial Crisis: The budget deficit surged to almost 10 percent of GDP and remained above 8 percent of GDP for the next two years.
• 2021 Covid Recession: The deficit reached an all-time high when outlays surged (above 30% of GDP) during the Covid recession.
• Composition Trends (Fig. 30.3): Among receipts, personal income taxes and Social Security taxes are the biggest shares; among outlays, transfer payments grew sharply while expenditure on goods and services trended down as a share of GDP.

Deficit vs. Debt & State/Local Sector · Fig. 30.4 (Slides 19–21)

Government Debt, Debt-to-GDP Ratio & State and Local Budgets

Government debt is the total amount the government has borrowed: the sum of past deficits minus past surpluses. When the government has a budget deficit, it borrows (increasing debt); when it has a budget surplus, it makes loan repayments (decreasing debt).

• Deficit vs. Debt: The deficit is an annual flow, whereas debt is the total amount owed. A persistent budget deficit leads to increased borrowing → larger interest payments → an even larger deficit.
• Debt-to-GDP History (Fig. 30.4): Peaked in World War II (~120% of GDP); shrank until ~1980 due to surpluses and fast economic growth; rose in 1980s–1990s from deficits and slow growth; shrank around 2000 from surpluses; surged after 2008 and jumped in the Covid-19 pandemic (>130% of GDP).
• State and Local Budgets: Total government includes federal plus state and local governments. In Fiscal 2018, federal outlays were $4,094 billion and state/local outlays were a further $3,600 billion (mostly public schools, colleges, and universities at $1,400 billion; local police and fire services; and roads).

Government Debt = Σ Past Deficits − Σ Past Surpluses

ليش؟ كيف يؤدي العجز المستمر في الميزانية إلى تفاقم العجز تلقائياً في السنوات التالية؟

عشان العجز المستمر يجبر الحكومة على زيادة الاقتراض ← فيرتفع الدين العام ← فتزيد مدفوعات فوائد الدين (Debt interest) ضمن النفقات ← فيتسع العجز أكثر!

EXAM TRAP · فخ الاختبار

Do not confuse deficit (annual outlays minus receipts) with debt (cumulative sum of past deficits minus past surpluses). Also, fast GDP growth shrinks the debt-to-GDP ratio even without paying off every dollar of debt.

How an Income Tax Lowers Employment and Potential GDP

Fiscal policy has important effects on employment, potential GDP, and aggregate supply, called supply-side effects:

1. Full Employment & Potential GDP: Equilibrium in the labor market determines full employment (300 billion hours/year at $40/hour); together with the production function (PF), it determines potential GDP ($30 trillion).
2. Income Tax Reduces Labor Supply: An income tax decreases the after-tax wage rate, shifting the labor supply curve leftward/upward from LS to LS + tax.
3. The Tax Wedge: The before-tax real wage rate rises (from $40 to $50/hour) while the after-tax real wage rate falls (from $40 to $30/hour). The gap between them ($20/hour) is the income tax wedge, and equilibrium employment decreases from 300 to 200 billion hours/year.
4. Lower Potential GDP & AS: Along the production function (PF), fewer labor hours reduce potential GDP from $30 trillion to $25 trillion and decrease aggregate supply (LAS and SAS shift left).

Tax Wedge = Before-Tax Wage ($50) − After-Tax Wage ($30) = $20/hr  |  L: 300B → 200B ⇒ Potential GDP: $30T → $25T
مثال بالعربي + ملاحظات الدفتر

احفظ أرقام الرسم Fig. 30.5 بالكامل: قبل الضريبة كان الأجر التوازني $40/ساعة والتوظيف 300 مليار ساعة والناتج المحتمل $30 تريليون. بعد فرض ضريبة الدخل ينزاح LS إلى LS + tax، فيرتفع الأجر قبل الضريبة إلى $50 ويهبط الأجر بعد الضريبة إلى $30 (الفجوة الضريبية = $20)، وينخفض التوظيف إلى 200 مليار ساعة والناتج المحتمل إلى $25 تريليون!

ليش؟ لماذا تسبب ضريبة الدخل انخفاضاً في العرض الكلي (AS) وليس الطلب فقط؟

عشان لأنها تقلل الأجر الصافي بعد الضريبة (After-tax real wage) فتخفض عرض العمل (LS) وساعات التوظيف، مما يقلل الطاقة الإنتاجية القصوى للاقتصاد (Potential GDP).

EXAM TRAP · فخ الاختبار

An income tax shifts LS leftward (to LS + tax), NOT LD. The before-tax wage rises ($40 → $50) while the after-tax wage falls ($40 → $30).

Consumption Tax & Capital Income Tax · Fig. 30.6 (Slides 29–32)

Taxes on Expenditure & Taxes on Capital Income

• Taxes on Expenditure Add to the Tax Wedge: A tax on consumption raises the prices paid for goods and services, which is equivalent to a cut in the real wage rate. For example, with a 25% income tax and a 10% consumption tax, the total tax wedge is 35%, so $1 earned buys only 65¢ worth of goods and services.
• Taxes on Capital Income Reduce Saving & Investment (Fig. 30.6): Saving and investment depend on the real after-tax interest rate (real interest rate minus income tax paid on interest income). Because taxes are levied on the nominal interest rate, the true tax on interest income depends on the inflation rate.
• In the loanable funds market (Fig. 30.6), a tax on capital income shifts supply of loanable funds leftward (SLF → SLF + tax). The real interest rate rises (3% → 4%) and the after-tax interest rate falls (3% → 1%), creating a 3% tax wedge. Saving and investment decrease ($2.0T → $1.8T), slowing real GDP growth.

25% Income Tax + 10% Consumption Tax = 35% Tax Wedge ($1 → 65¢)  |  Capital Tax: r (3% → 4%), After-tax r (3% → 1%)
Tax Rate vs. Tax Revenue · Fig. 30.7 (Slides 33–35)

Tax Revenues and the Laffer Curve

The Laffer curve is the relationship between the tax rate and the amount of tax revenue collected. At a high enough tax rate, a rise in the rate decreases revenue because people work less and find ways to avoid the tax:

• Below T* (e.g., United States, United Kingdom): A rise in the tax rate increases tax revenue.
• At T* (e.g., near France?): Tax revenue is maximized.
• Above T*: A rise in the tax rate decreases tax revenue.

Tax Rate < T* ⇒ Revenue ↑  |  Tax Rate = T* ⇒ Max Revenue  |  Tax Rate > T* ⇒ Revenue ↓
مثال بالعربي + ملاحظات الدفتر

نقطة جوهرية في السلايد 30: الضرائب تُفرض على سعر الفائدة الاسمي (Nominal interest rate) وليس الحقيقي؛ لذلك كلما ارتفع التضخم (Inflation rate) زادت الضريبة الفعلية على عائد المدخرات وانخفض العائد الحقيقي بعد الضريبة (Real after-tax interest rate)!

ليش؟ لماذا تنخفض الإيرادات الضريبية إذا رفعت الحكومة معدل الضريبة فوق النقطة T* على منحنى لافر؟

عشان لأن معدل الضريبة المرتفع جداً يُضعف حافز العمل والادخار والاستثمار ويدفع الناس للتهرب الضريبي، فينكمش الوعاء الضريبي بأكثر من زيادة نسبة الضريبة.

EXAM TRAP · فخ الاختبار

Taxes on interest income are levied on the nominal interest rate, NOT the real interest rate—so a higher inflation rate increases the true tax burden on saving.

Generational Accounting & Present Value · Slides 36–38

Generational Accounting & Present Value

• Generational accounting: An accounting system that measures the lifetime tax burden and benefits of each generation.
• Present value (PV): An amount of money that, if invested today, will grow to equal a given future amount when the interest that it earns is taken into account (used to compare taxes paid during working years with benefits received after retirement).
• Textbook Example: At an interest rate of 5% a year, $1,000 invested today grows to $11,467 after 50 years. Therefore, the present value (in 2021) of $11,467 in 2071 is $1,000.

Future Value = PV × (1 + r)n  ⇒  $1,000 × (1.05)50 = $11,467
Social Security Time Bomb & International Debt · Slides 39–44

Fiscal Imbalance, Generational Imbalance & International Debt

• Social Security Time Bomb: Baby boomers began collecting Social Security pensions in 2008 and became eligible for Medicare in 2011. By 2030, all baby boomers reach retirement age and the population supported by Social Security doubles.
• Fiscal Imbalance: The present value of the government's commitments to pay benefits minus the present value of its tax revenues. Economists Gokhale and Smetters estimated the U.S. fiscal imbalance at $68 trillion in 2014—4 times U.S. total production in 2014 ($17 trillion).
• Four Alternatives to Eliminate the Imbalance (from 2003): 1. Raise income taxes by 69%, OR 2. Raise Social Security taxes by 95%, OR 3. Cut Social Security benefits by 56%, OR 4. Cut federal discretionary spending.
• Generational Imbalance: The division of the fiscal imbalance between current and future generations, assuming the current generation enjoys existing levels of taxes and benefits.
• International Debt (March 2021): U.S. net debt to the rest of the world was $33.4 trillion. Of that total, $7 trillion was U.S. government borrowing (about 26% of total U.S. government debt), and $17.8 trillion was U.S. corporate borrowing (bonds and equities).

Fiscal Stimulus & Automatic Fiscal Policy

A fiscal stimulus is the use of fiscal policy to increase production and employment. Fiscal stimulus can be either automatic or discretionary.

Automatic fiscal policy is a fiscal policy action triggered by the state of the economy, with no government action. Two budget items change automatically:
1. Tax Revenues (Induced Taxes): Government sets tax rates, while tax dollars paid depend on incomes (real GDP). In a recession, incomes fall so tax revenue automatically falls; in an expansion, incomes rise so tax revenue automatically rises.
2. Needs-Tested Spending: Transfer payments to qualified people and businesses depend on the state of the economy. In a recession, unemployment rises so needs-tested spending automatically increases; in an expansion, unemployment falls so needs-tested spending automatically decreases.

Structural and Cyclical Surpluses or Deficits

• Structural surplus or deficit: The budget balance that would occur if the economy were at full employment and real GDP equaled potential GDP.
• Cyclical surplus or deficit: The actual surplus or deficit minus the structural surplus or deficit—that is, the surplus or deficit that occurs purely because real GDP does not equal potential GDP.

• Fig. 30.9(b) Structural Balance (Real GDP = Potential GDP): At full employment of $19T (Point A) → Structural deficit; at $20T (Point B) → Balanced budget; at $21T (Point C) → Structural surplus.

Discretionary Fiscal Policy & Multipliers · Slide 11 (PDF) & Slides 59–62, 65 (PPTX)

Government Expenditure Multiplier vs. Tax Multiplier

Discretionary fiscal policy is a policy action initiated by an act of the government (Congress). Most of it works through aggregate demand via two multipliers:

1. Government Expenditure Multiplier: The quantitative effect of a change in government expenditure on real GDP.
• Multiplier Effect (> 1): Government spending is part of aggregate expenditure, so real GDP rises → incomes and consumption rise → AD increases. Alone, the multiplier would exceed 1.
• Crowding-Out Effect (< 1): More government borrowing raises the real interest rate. Higher borrowing costs reduce private investment, partly offsetting the increase in government spending. Alone, this pushes the multiplier below 1.
• Consensus: The crowding-out effect dominates, so the government expenditure multiplier is less than 1 (< 1).

2. Tax Multiplier: The quantitative effect of a change in taxes on aggregate demand / real GDP.
• Demand Side: The demand-side effects of a tax cut are likely to be smaller than those of an equivalent increase in government expenditure.
• Supply Side: A tax cut narrows tax wedges and increases the supply of labor and supply of loanable funds, increasing aggregate supply.
• Net Result: Supply-side effects probably dominate demand-side effects and make the tax multiplier larger than the government expenditure multiplier.

Magnitude & Three Time Lags · Slide 13 (PDF) & Slides 66–67 (PPTX)

Why Discretionary Fiscal Policy Is Risky

1. Uncertainty About the Magnitude of Stimulus:
• Multipliers are uncertain: Economists disagree on their exact size because there is too little empirical evidence to pin it down.
• Right size is unclear: Without knowing the multiplier, the government cannot determine the exact stimulus needed to close a given output gap.
• The output gap is unknown: Potential GDP cannot be observed directly, so the actual output gap can only be estimated with error.

2. Three Time Lags Hamper Discretionary Fiscal Policy:
1. Recognition lag: The time it takes to figure out that fiscal policy action is needed.
2. Law-making lag: The time it takes Congress (the government) to pass the laws needed to change taxes or spending.
3. Impact lag: The time from passing a tax or spending change until its effect on real GDP is felt.

Total Delay = Recognition Lag + Law-Making Lag + Impact Lag ⇒ Discretionary Policy Is Risky
الفجوة الزمنية (Time Lag) التعريف الدقيق (Exact Definition) سبب التأخير (Why It Happens)
1. Recognition Lag (فجوة التعرف) الوقت اللازم لاكتشاف أن الاقتصاد يحتاج إلى تدخل مالي تأخر صدور بيانات الناتج المحلي والبطالة وتأكيد وجود ركود
2. Law-Making Lag (فجوة التشريع) الوقت اللازم للبرلمان (Congress) لإقرار قوانين الضرائب أو الإنفاق المناقشات السياسية والتعديلات والتصويت بين السلطات
3. Impact Lag (فجوة الأثر) الوقت من إقرار القانون حتى ظهور أثره الفعلي على Real GDP بطء تنفيذ المشاريع وتعديل خطط إنفاق الأسر والشركات
مثال بالعربي + ملاحظات الدفتر

احفظ الترتيب الزمني الصحيح للفجوات الثلاث: أولاً تكتشف الحكومة المشكلة (Recognition lag)، ثانياً تقرّ البرلمان/الكونغرس القانون (Law-making lag)، ثالثاً يظهر أثر القانون في الاقتصاد الحقيقي (Impact lag).

ليش؟ لماذا تُعد السياسة المالية التقديرية (Discretionary) محفوفة بالمخاطر مقارنة بالتلقائية؟

عشان بسبب عدم اليقين في حجم المضاعف، وصعوبة قياس فجوة الناتج بدقة، بالإضافة إلى الفجوات الزمنية الثلاث التي قد تجعل التحفيز يصل في وقت متأخر فيحول الركود إلى تضخم!

CONCEPT 09

Income Tax & Tax Wedge (Fig. 30.5)

اضغط لقلب البطاقة · Flip
المعنى والقانون

ضريبة الدخل تزيح LS يساراً (LS + tax)، فترفع الأجر قبل الضريبة ($40→$50) وتخفض الأجر بعد الضريبة ($40→$30) بفجوة $20 وتخفض التوظيف والناتج المحتمل ($30T→$25T).

Income tax shifts LS left: before-tax wage rises ($40→$50), after-tax wage falls ($40→$30), tax wedge = $20, L falls (300B→200B), Potential GDP falls ($30T→$25T).
CONCEPT 10

Consumption Tax Wedge

اضغط لقلب البطاقة · Flip
المعنى والقانون

ضريبة الاستهلاك ترفع الأسعار فتكافئ خفض الأجر الحقيقي: 25% ضريبة دخل + 10% ضريبة استهلاك = 35% فجوة ضريبية ($1 يشتري 65¢ فقط).

25% income tax + 10% consumption tax = 35% tax wedge; $1 earned buys only 65 cents' worth of goods and services.
CONCEPT 11

Capital Income Tax (Fig. 30.6)

اضغط لقلب البطاقة · Flip
المعنى والقانون

تزيح عرض الأموال القابلة للإقراض (SLF) يساراً، فترفع الفائدة (3%→4%) وتخفض الفائدة بعد الضريبة (3%→1%) وتخفض الادخار والاستثمار ($2.0T→$1.8T).

Tax on capital income shifts SLF left: real interest rate rises (3%→4%), after-tax rate falls (3%→1%), tax wedge = 3%, S and I fall ($2.0T→$1.8T).
CONCEPT 12

Laffer Curve & Peak T* (Fig. 30.7)

اضغط لقلب البطاقة · Flip
المعنى والقانون

توضح العلاقة بين معدل الضريبة والحصيلة الضريبية: تحت T* رفع الضريبة يزيد الحصيلة، عند T* تُعظّم الحصيلة، فوق T* رفع الضريبة يخفض الحصيلة.

Below T*, higher tax rates raise revenue; at T*, revenue is maximized; above T*, higher tax rates reduce revenue due to tax avoidance and less work.
CONCEPT 13

Generational Accounting & Present Value

اضغط لقلب البطاقة · Flip
المعنى والقانون

نظام يقيس عبء الضرائب والمزايا مدى الحياة لكل جيل؛ والقيمة الحالية (PV) تقارن مبالغ اليوم بالمستقبل ($1,000 بفائدة 5% تصبح $11,467 بعد 50 سنة).

Generational accounting measures lifetime taxes and benefits per generation. At 5% interest, $1,000 today grows to $11,467 in 50 years.
CONCEPT 14

اضغط لقلب البطاقة · Flip

تضاعف المتقاعدين بحلول 2030 يخلق اختلالاً مالياً (PV الالتزامات − PV الضرائب) قدره $68 تريليون في 2014 (4 أضعاف GDP البالغ $17T).

Fiscal imbalance = PV(promised benefits) − PV(tax revenues) = $68 trillion in 2014 (4 times GDP of $17T).
CONCEPT 15

Generational Imbalance & Foreign Debt

Generational imbalance divides fiscal imbalance between current and future generations. U.S. net international debt = $33.4T ($7T government = 26%).

Fiscal policy action triggered by the state of the economy with no government action, via tax revenues and needs-tested spending.
CONCEPT 17

Transfer payments to qualified people and businesses: increase automatically in a recession and decrease automatically in an expansion.
CONCEPT 18

The budget balance that would occur if the economy were at full employment and real GDP equaled potential GDP.

Cyclical balance = Actual balance − Structural balance; occurs purely because real GDP does not equal potential GDP.

CONCEPT 21

Government Expenditure Multiplier (< 1)

اضغط لقلب البطاقة · Flip
المعنى والقانون

أثر المضاعف (> 1) يقابله أثر المزاحمة Crowding-Out (< 1) نتيجة ارتفاع الفائدة وانخفاض الاستثمار؛ والإجماع أن المزاحمة تطغى فالمضاعف < 1.

Multiplier effect (>1) vs. crowding-out effect (<1) as borrowing raises r and cuts investment; consensus: crowding-out dominates, multiplier < 1.
CONCEPT 22

Tax Multiplier (Demand vs. Supply Side)

اضغط لقلب البطاقة · Flip
المعنى والقانون

أثر خفض الضرائب على الطلب أصغر من الإنفاق الحكومي، لكن أثره الإيجابي على العرض (LS و SLF) يجعله أكبر من مضاعف الإنفاق في المحصلة.

Demand-side effect is smaller than ΔG, but supply-side boost to labor and loanable funds makes the tax multiplier larger than the expenditure multiplier.

CONCEPT 24

عدم يقين المضاعف وفجوة الناتج + 3 فجوات زمنية: (1) التعرف Recognition، (2) التشريع Law-making، (3) الأثر Impact تجعل السياسة التقديرية خطرة.

Uncertain multipliers & unknown output gap + three lags (Recognition lag, Law-making lag, Impact lag) make discretionary fiscal policy risky.

Correct: A. Government debt is the total amount the government has borrowed: the sum of past deficits minus past surpluses.
8
In Fig. 30.4, which two factors caused the U.S. debt-to-GDP ratio to shrink dramatically between World War II and the 1970s?
Correct: B. Surplus and fast GDP growth shrank the debt-to-GDP ratio after World War II.
9
In Fiscal 2018, federal outlays were about $4,094 billion and state and local outlays were $3,600 billion. What was the largest category of state spending ($1,400 billion)?
Correct: C. Most state expenditures ($1,400 billion of $3,600 billion) went to public schools, colleges, and universities.
10
In Fig. 30.5, imposing an income tax shifts the labor supply curve from LS to LS + tax. What happens to the before-tax wage, after-tax wage, and potential GDP?
Correct: D. The income tax creates a $20 tax wedge, lowering employment from 300B to 200B hours and potential GDP from $30T to $25T.
11
If the income tax rate is 25% and the consumption tax rate is 10%, what is the total tax wedge and how much purchasing power does $1 of earnings have?
Correct: B. Consumption taxes add to the tax wedge (25% + 10% = 35%), so $1 earned buys only 65 cents of goods and services.
12
Why does a higher inflation rate increase the true tax burden on interest income?
Correct: C. Taxes depend on the nominal interest rate, so higher inflation raises the effective tax on real interest income.
13
In Fig. 30.6, a tax on capital income shifts SLF leftward to SLF + tax. What are the resulting equilibrium values in the loanable funds market?
Correct: A. SLF shifts left, driving a 3% tax wedge between the 4% market rate and 1% after-tax rate while cutting investment/saving to $1.8T.
14
On the Laffer curve (Fig. 30.7), what occurs at the tax rate T*?
Correct: C. At T*, tax revenue reaches its peak; below T* raising rates increases revenue, and above T* raising rates decreases revenue.
15
If the annual interest rate is 5%, $1,000 invested today grows to $11,467 in 50 years. What does this tell us about present value?
Correct: B. Present value discounts a future sum ($11,467 in 50 years) back to today's equivalent ($1,000 at 5%).
16
According to Gokhale and Smetters, how large was the U.S. fiscal imbalance in 2014 compared to total U.S. production ($17 trillion)?
Correct: D. The fiscal imbalance was estimated at $68 trillion in 2014, which was 4 times U.S. GDP of $17 trillion.
17
How is 'generational imbalance' defined?
Correct: A. Generational imbalance measures how the fiscal imbalance is split between current and future generations.
18
In March 2021, U.S. net debt to the rest of the world was $33.4 trillion. Approximately what percentage of total U.S. government debt was held abroad ($7 trillion)?
Correct: C. Of the $33.4T net foreign debt, $7 trillion was U.S. government borrowing—about 26% of total U.S. government debt.
19
During a recession, how do automatic stabilizers affect the government budget without any action by Congress?
Correct: B. Lower incomes reduce tax receipts while higher unemployment raises needs-tested transfer spending, providing automatic stimulus.
20
In Fig. 30.9(b), if real GDP equals potential GDP at $21 trillion (Point C), the budget balance is a:
Correct: D. Because Real GDP = Potential GDP at $21T and receipts exceed outlays, the surplus is a structural surplus.
21
In 2020 (Fig. 30.10), the actual U.S. budget deficit was $3,213 billion and the cyclical deficit was $3,268 billion. What was the structural budget balance?
Correct: A. Cyclical (−$3,268B) = Actual (−$3,213B) − Structural (+$55B), indicating a $55 billion structural surplus.
22
Why is the consensus among economists that the government expenditure multiplier is less than 1?
Correct: C. Government borrowing raises the real interest rate and crowds out investment; consensus is that crowding-out dominates so the multiplier is < 1.
23
Comparing the tax multiplier and the government expenditure multiplier, economists conclude that:
Correct: B. Although a tax cut has a smaller demand-side effect, its supply-side expansion of labor and loanable funds makes the tax multiplier larger.
24
In Fig. 30.11, when fiscal stimulus closes a $1 trillion recessionary gap starting at Point A ($19T, P = 110), what is the final full-employment equilibrium?
Correct: C. AD shifts to AD1 and the price level rises along SAS from 110 to 115, restoring potential GDP ($20T) at Point C.
25
What is the 'recognition lag' in discretionary fiscal policy?
Correct: A. Recognition lag is the time required to identify that the economy needs fiscal policy action.
